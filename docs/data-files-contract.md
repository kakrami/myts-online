# Connected-history source datasets and evidence

## Owner workflow and archive compatibility

Settings → Data files and Trace Manage → Import data / Export data / Export evidence operate on one logical app team. A selected season is not required. Only the authenticated owner can list, create, upload, reopen or download these jobs; viewer links cannot use them. The overview shows recent jobs, their progress and their expiry. The API provides bounded, stable pagination. A staged upload can be resumed with the original ZIP; completed exports can be downloaded again.

New archives declare `scope.scope_kind: connected_history` and archive format `7.2`. The format is the supported source-graph contract. The producer `release` is informational: another application patch can import a supported-format archive. Unknown formats are rejected before staging. Evidence archives (`myts-source-evidence`) are for review and cannot be imported; datasets use `myts-source-dataset`.

The uncompressed ZIP contains ordered `segments/000000.json` entries and a final `manifest.json`. The manifest declares native source teams, portable source/account labels, creation and source-revision metadata, and each segment's section, row count and SHA-256. ZIP CRC checks cover every entry. The reader validates the directory, filenames, entry bounds, CRC and SHA-256 without inflating or loading the complete ZIP.

Already-persisted `legacy_season` jobs retain their original season, stored manifests, checksums, ZIP bytes, IDs and checkpoints. Their original single-season/existing-record import checks and final season projection continue to apply. Existing legacy staging can resume. New uploads do not start legacy jobs, and there is no general old-format import adapter. New connected-history jobs use a nullable season plus explicit scope kind, not a fabricated season.

## Saved scope and authority

An export includes all saved observed history and undated pending observations reachable from the team's active typed source connections. Fixed-season roots and explicit season exclusions still restrict that scope. Existing public-source correspondence may extend reachability; file content cannot create that correspondence. Competition-only registrations, divisions and competitions are retained even when no fixture has yet been saved.

Typed sections include native teams and players, competitions/divisions/standings, schedules, rosters, logistics, attendance, Trace members/clips/tracking/statistics/heatmaps and period-qualified roster snapshots. Private account references replace installation account UUIDs. Owner sessions, provider credentials, Coach material, deployment settings and recursive secret fields are excluded. Canonical approvals and owner corrections remain target-local.

An import requires matching, already-authorized target roots and already-known private provider/native-account identities. It may restore a wholly missing dependent graph into a team with no derived seasons, without a provider fetch. Saved data under a disconnected account can remain locally usable when that owner/root scope is valid. This does not reactivate the account, grant upstream collection, add account ownership, create identity links or trust file-supplied verification/completeness claims.

Source writes carry `file_import` provenance. Existing provider observations win over file observations; owner identity decisions and corrections are untouched. Real provider confirmation can subsequently supersede imported display authority. Native ID, provider/account, root, event/team side and tracking-member checks remain mandatory. Future observation timestamps are rejected.

### Historical roster snapshots

`source_roster_snapshots` carries portable team/player references, optional event reference, original season year, validity interval, name, jersey, position and observation metadata. The year can preserve an imported historical roster even without a dated event. Conflicting dates/year or fixed/excluded target scope are rejected.

Applied rows have the explicit `imported_snapshot` basis and `preserved_uncertain` certainty. They cannot invent event dates, verified attendance/participation, source resolution or provider continuity. Native/local preserved snapshots and owner overrides retain stronger authority. Imported audit rows remain after later provider confirmation or event-date correction. Ordinary retired, nonexcluded derived membership can be reactivated at the current root generation; fixed or excluded membership cannot be widened. Settled player reads label imported, preserved and provider-sourced labels separately, and pending reconciliation remains visible.

## Capture, validation and recovery

Exports are resumable background jobs. Each invocation serializes at most ten ordered rows; oversized rows reduce the page length. Captures fence roots/generations/scopes/exclusions, relevant account generations, derived membership and exact serialized row clocks, including joined logistics, heatmaps and roster evidence. Source changes affecting the captured graph fail the export rather than publish a mixed snapshot. The signature is checked before/after descriptor preparation and atomically at creation/publication. A publication token fences every stored part, directory entry and checkpoint. Incomplete ZIPs are never downloadable. After the manifest closes, directory construction and downloads use immutable saved parts.

Uploads are first checksummed and fully shape-checked without source writes, then staged immutably. The browser must stay open until upload finishes. A server-created strategy marker records that every staged segment passed the current shape proof; client-supplied markers are ignored. Explicit commit queues one archive identity/completeness pass, then one complete graph predicate per supported native table (at most twenty phases). No source apply starts until every segment belongs to a validated table. Auxiliary duplicates use the source writer’s native identity components, including account/team, period, side and chunk/page where applicable; noncanonical portable reference aliases are rejected. This permits missing events, players, divisions and competitions without prior provider collection, while rejecting a bad late segment before source writes. Persisted connected-history jobs without the server marker conservatively retain full per-segment checksum, shape and graph validation; an interrupted older upload cannot opt into cached shape proof.

After all segments validate, dependency-ordered pages apply through the existing bounded source writer. Reusing the staging graph proof requires the exact persisted manifest, immutable segment set/bytes, all-validated status, expected apply cursor and counts. Those conditions and the current selected-root/account/scope signature are rechecked atomically with source writes and checkpoint progress. Adding an unrelated root does not invalidate an unchanged selected import scope. Changing a selected root generation, relevant exclusion/account generation, or target lifecycle does. Lease loss or a failed precondition cannot partially commit the page.

Committed work continues through the normal queue, alarms and connection recovery with the browser closed. A durable import-recovery marker is saved before collection. Intermediate import pages coalesce their own recovery; success, failure, blocked/retry backoff, or idle after an interrupted final commit releases already-saved source data for reconciliation. Connection recovery becomes durable before the import marker is cleared. Explicit owner wakes and provider/projection commits still recover immediately, and concurrent imports retain their recovery obligation. Eight real-coordinator/SQLite regressions cover this ordering and behavior. New imports dirty typed roots and projections; they do not inherit the old single-season projection child. Status distinguishes queued, validating, applying, source-saved/reconciling, complete and reconciliation-needs-attention. A completed source import is not presented as a finished team view while reconciliation remains pending.

## Capacity and retention

- ZIP size: at most 64 MiB, including ZIP/manifest/directory overhead.
- Staged JSON payloads also have a server-side aggregate 64 MiB bound.
- Manifest and each segment: at most 768,000 bytes.
- At most 4,096 segments, ten rows per segment, 32 native roots and 16 private accounts. Combined byte/metadata limits may bind before an individual count limit.
- Stored ZIP parts: at most 64,000 bytes each. A download read returns at most 256 parts and at most 4 MiB of stored bytes, bounding both statement count and BLOB expansion.
- Job lists: at most 20 entries per page, with timestamp/ID pagination.
- Files and staging jobs expire after 24 hours. Expiry removes transfer artifacts, not source observations already imported.

Capacity failures do not silently truncate records. Team lifecycle checks guard actual atomic artifact writes and prevent reads/publications after deletion starts. Team-local artifacts are removed without deleting shared source facts.
